# Optimization history

This is the repository's single history of attempted performance work. Entries
retain only the hypothesis, disposition, and durable lesson. They do not define
current behavior; current contracts live in architecture and subsystem docs.

`Accepted` means the useful mechanism remains or its result became product
policy. `Rejected` means the candidate was removed or disabled. `Measured`
means the work improved understanding without justifying a product change.

## General gadget forwarding

| Attempt | Disposition and lesson |
| --- | --- |
| Template-owned adjacent scalar result forwarding | Accepted as the default policy; `BuildOptions` can still disable it per program for controls. Shared assembly operand contracts select a forwarding variant from the decoded entry rather than from an opcode-pair whitelist. Dependent scalar probes ran two to three times faster while full workloads showed broad coverage rather than a general speedup, and a mechanism control must match both the code layout and the data addresses of its arm. |
| Template-owned adjacent SIMD/FP result forwarding | Accepted as a default beside scalar forwarding. Natural-v2 contracts extend the shared SIMD/FP templates without new hot-path producer copies. Geekbench gains were small against shared-host variation, so correct forwarding coverage is not a broad speedup claim. |
| Combined scalar/SIMD dependency coverage | Measured with both policies enabled. Broad contract coverage did not imply frequent adjacent dependencies: SIMD added only a few points of entered-instruction coverage. Coverage counts execution-weighted opportunities, not retired reads or speedup; the diagnostic was later removed (80e8e201). |
| Preserve results across opposite-domain gadgets | Measured, not enabled. Preserving results across audited nonclobbering opposite-domain entries added about a tenth of a point of coverage, too little to justify separate preservation contracts. |

## 4 KiB page path

| ID | Attempt | Disposition and lesson |
| --- | --- | --- |
| G263 | Give the 4 KiB software TLB the 16 KiB geometry: 16,384 two-way sets indexed directly by page number | Rejected. The one-way folded table of 8,192 entries (256 KiB per vCPU) became four times larger and two-way, with the crossing test folded into the tag compare and one probe shape in both profiles. The M4 MacBook Air stayed level on 4 KiB Geekbench (single-core 1.003, multi-core 1.008), but the iPad mini 6 lost 2% single-core in every run (0.980; multi-core 0.994). Both hosts moved the same workloads: Navigation lost 12% single-core (0.883 iPad, 0.876 MacBook Air) and Structure from Motion 6%, while Text Processing gained 4%. Object Detection's 11% single-core gain on the MacBook Air was noise: that workload scores about 230 or about 330 on the MacBook Air in any build, and one run's mode moves the single-core score by 2%. The likely cause, not proven, is the table's cache footprint: each page now owns a 64-byte line where the one-way table packs two pages to a line, so a pointer-chasing working set pays more in cache misses than the extra reach saves. G265 then doubled the one-way table instead and kept two pages to a line. The probe test written for it, `arm64-tlb-probe-set`, stayed. |
| G264 | Fill a 4 KiB TLB miss's whole 16 KiB group: one static-MMU lookup returns all four pages and the miss installs each mapped one | Rejected: single-core Geekbench stayed level on the M1 (1.001 over four quads). A counting build showed that two thirds of 4 KiB misses (about 30 million in one single-core Geekbench run, against about one million at 16 KiB) find another page of their group already cached, but installing the other three pages evicts live entries from the direct-mapped table: misses rose 43% and the fill path's host samples doubled. Installing them only into free slots evicts nothing but finds almost no room: flushes are rare, 98% of misses land in an occupied slot, and it installed fewer than 1% as many pages as there were misses. The 4 KiB miss stream is capacity and conflict, not cold. |
| G265 | Give the 4 KiB software TLB 16,384 one-way entries indexed directly by page number, with the page-crossing test moved to the miss path | Accepted. The table doubles to 512 KiB per vCPU but still packs two pages to a 64-byte line, and the hit path loses the index fold and the front crossing test: the key is the last byte's page, a direct index never puts a page and its successor in one slot, so a crossing access always misses and the miss path finds it. On the iPad mini 6, 4 KiB Geekbench gained 4.0% single-core and 3.8% multi-core over four ABBA pairs, with every run of the new table above every run of the old and every workload ahead (Navigation 7.5% single-core, where G263 lost 12%); peak phys_footprint fell 0.6%. Aquarium on the M4 MacBook Air rose 2.3% over eight pairs. A counting build put single-core fills at about 12 million per run, against 24 to 31 million with the folded table and about one million at 16 KiB. Measured directly against 16 KiB over four ABBA pairs, 4 KiB Geekbench now scores 1.008 single-core and 1.003 multi-core on the iPad (0.969 and 0.961 before) and 0.997 and 0.987 on the MacBook Air (0.962 and 0.949 before), with Navigation 9 to 10% faster at 4 KiB than at 16 KiB on both. 16 KiB emitted code is byte-identical. This supports, without proving, G263's explanation: the same direct index with half its entries and a line shared by neighbouring pages wins where one line per page lost. |
| G266 | Fault 4 KiB anonymous writes in aligned 16 KiB blocks, and keep the per-CPU page lists at their minimum high mark | Rejected. Measured against the folded 4 KiB table before G265, it moved iPad single-core +0.7 percent, multi-core +1.1 and peak footprint -1.6, M4 MacBook Air multi-core +1.6 with single-core level, and M1 single-core +0.7; a first version without the page-list limit held up to 1 GiB of free pages on an 8 GiB guest. It gives back the sparse anonymous footprint that 4 KiB pages buy, four times finer than 16 KiB blocks, which Android guests depend on most; it would be a shared kernel topic that rebuilds every line; and the page-list limit departs from upstream. A fault-batching gain of about a percent is not worth undoing the page size's memory advantage. |
| G269 | Publish a kernel write into guest code only once x86 code may run | Accepted. On 4 KiB pages every uaccess write is copied back into the guest page, and the copy published a code change whenever the page was guest code. With an executable stack nearly every system call that writes its result there (`clock_gettime()` into a stack timespec) published one, and each publication advanced the gadget cache's epoch and reset the shared space's links. arm64 and AArch32 code see such a write only after their own cache maintenance, as on 16 KiB pages, so now only an x86 dispatch turns the publication on. Per Aquarium frame on the M1 Ultra (2 vCPUs), slot validations fell from 12,400 to 2,800 (16 KiB: 1,450) and epoch advances from 48 to 36 (19); 4 KiB Aquarium rose 6.8% over four runs per arm against main (5.8% in an earlier series). A path only one page profile takes can carry a side effect the other never pays: compare both profiles' event counts, not only their times. |
| G270 | Fill a gadget TLB miss through the executor's entered execution context | Accepted. A miss resolved the thread's address space through the registry and read the thread's active generation, and each Darwin `thread_local` access is a `_tlv_get_addr()` call: sampling the process-switch probe found more busy time there than in the page lookup. The entered context already holds the generation's state, so the miss looks the page up in it. `guest_process_switch_loop` ran 32% faster at both page sizes, every run of the new path below every run of the old, and `guest_mmu_miss_walk_loop` 3 to 4%; Aquarium stayed within its noise. On a Darwin hot path, pass a resolved object down rather than looking it up through `thread_local` state per call. |
| G271 | Attribute the 4 KiB Aquarium gap | Measured on the M1 Ultra with Time Profiler, 2 vCPUs, four runs per arm. On main, 4 KiB spent 14.3% more vCPU time per frame than 16 KiB (fps ratio 0.900): gadget execution 41% of the difference, the program cache 29% (revalidation after G269's publications), TLB fills 11% (twice as many: a refill after an address-space switch costs per page) and the guest kernel 14%; no extra page-boundary block splitting was found. With G269 and G270 the difference fell to 3.3% (0.978). Of what remains, the fills are a fifth; two 4 KiB-only kernel paths are a quarter: a uaccess bridge call per page (`darwin_uml_vm_sync_from_backing()`) that shared kernel aliases made redundant, and init_mm slot synchronization over a whole purged vmap range; gadget execution is within its run spread. |
| G273 | Fill an entered context's TLB miss from a lock-free radix index of its generation's mappings | Accepted. The fill took the mapping map's reader lock, a cache line every vCPU writes, and then missed on a hash node per page, and a 4 KiB guest refills about twice as often as a 16 KiB one. A radix table of atomic words with 64-page leaves, freed as they empty, is now read without a lock: every removal or change of a mapping already quiesces execution, so a reader never sees a stale word, and an absent one sends it to the map. On the Mac (Aquarium, 2 vCPUs) TLB fill time per frame fell from 1.7–2.6 to 0.6–0.7 ms at 4 KiB and from 1.0–1.6 to 0.3–0.5 ms at 16 KiB; the M4 MacBook Air gained 2.6% fps at 16 KiB (U 12.5/16) and held at 4 KiB. The index costs 29 bytes per mapped page at 4 KiB and 47 at 16 KiB against about 72 for the map; 512-page leaves filled as fast but cost 64 and 97. |
| G274 | Answer a 4 KiB uaccess backing sync under one lock | Accepted. `darwin_uml_vm_sync_from_backing()` runs for every page that kernel uaccess touches on 4 KiB pages, and it took the space's lock twice and the ownership registry's lock once although the page is nearly always one the space maps directly. One hold of the space's lock now answers it, and only a range reaching past the space's direct slots asks the registry. Its share of 4 KiB Aquarium vCPU time fell from 0.36% to 0.26%. The call itself stays, because a slot the space holds as a host-private shadow still has to be refilled from its backing. |
| G275 | Read the Darwin carrier CPU from the thread's TSD instead of calling `pthread_getspecific()` | Accepted, kernel release p4k1. Every use of `current` asked the carrier's pthread key through the PLT, 3.5% of all vCPU time on the iPad in Aquarium and 0.8% (16 KiB) to 1.5% (4 KiB) on the Mac. Kernel-owned state cannot answer instead: host signal handlers run kernel code on the sigaltstack, carriers run on native stacks, and XNU owns `TPIDR_EL0`. The kernel now keeps each carrier's CPU number in a second key and loads its word at `TPIDRRO_EL0` plus key × 8, behind a per-carrier check against `pthread_getspecific()` that turns the read off on any mismatch. A getpid loop fell 22%, and on the Mac the lookup's callers fell from about 1.45 to 0.35 ms per 4 KiB Aquarium frame; the iPad at 16 KiB moved 1.007 (U 9/16), inside its noise. An inline read was no faster on the loop and added 475 KB of kernel text, and an out-of-line read that went through the carrier's data pointer behind a stack frame was 6% slower on it. |
| G276 | Apply only changed `init_mm` PTEs in a kernel TLB sync | Accepted, kernel release p4k2. A kernel-range walk mapped every present PTE of its range again, and a vmalloc purge flushes the whole span between the areas it frees, which the vfree of vmapped kernel stacks and of seccomp's interpreted BPF programs triggers often. The walk now applies only PTEs that carry `_PAGE_NEEDSYNC`, on the 4 KiB profile only slots with such a quarter, and clears the bit by compare-and-exchange once applied; a kernel-mode fault marks its page first. A fork-burst loop (4 vCPUs, ABBA-BAAB) ran 38% faster at 4 KiB and 42% at 16 KiB, every new run below every old one. In Aquarium the `init_mm` sync is bimodal per 28-second window, about 0.4 ms per frame when a purge lands and near zero otherwise, which fps cannot resolve. |
| G277 | Invalidate arm64 translated code only over the changed range | Rejected before it was built. A counting build (Aquarium, 2 vCPUs, 2 GiB, two runs per profile) showed 4 KiB translating and validating no more than 16 KiB: 5.8 against 7.6 thousand translations and 6.5 against 6.2 thousand body validations per frame. Only epoch advances differ, 32 against 20 per frame, and all validation together costs at most 0.55 ms per frame, about 0.4% of vCPU time, which bounds what a precise invalidation could recover. |
| G278 | Plan no page for a batch unmap that names a page nothing holds | Accepted. A 4 KiB guest's TLB replay sends one unmap per absent PTE, and the host planned each page of every unmap before finding it empty: a counting build (Aquarium, 2 vCPUs, 2 GiB) found 14,700 such pages per 4 KiB frame against 5,700 at 16 KiB, and not one of them mapped, because the host removes a page when Linux invalidates it. An unmap now plans only pages its generation or an earlier operation of the batch holds. On the Mac the batch commit fell from 1.18 to 0.84 ms per 4 KiB frame (four and three runs), which whole-run vCPU time cannot resolve. |
| G279 | Send neighbouring Darwin user PTEs as one host call | Accepted, kernel release p4k4. A user replay made one host call per PTE: a counting build (Aquarium, 2 vCPUs, 2 GiB) found 4,200 maps and 14,200 unmaps per 4 KiB frame against 980 and 5,600 at 16 KiB, most of the unmaps in long runs of empty PTEs. The walk now sends a run of PTEs that are all absent, or that map consecutive offsets of one backing with one protection, as one call. 4 KiB maps fell by a third and unmaps by 97%, and the Mac's TLB sync fell from 1.96 to 1.52 ms per 4 KiB frame. With the topic as the only difference, Aquarium at 4 KiB was level on the M4 MacBook Air (1.002, eight runs per arm) and on the iPad (1.000, four). A dropped run cannot be observed, because an absent page faults back in; `guest-tlb-replay-runs` catches a run that merges protections or offsets. |
| G281 | Remove the 16 KiB guest page profile | Accepted, superseding G37. After the one-way 4 KiB TLB (G265) and the 4 KiB work that followed, the 4 KiB profile matched the 16 KiB one where users see it: iPad mini 6 Geekbench 4K/16K 1.008 single-core and 1.003 multi-core, and WebGL Aquarium 4K/16K 1.017 on the iPad, 0.988 on the M4 MacBook Air and 0.985 on the Mac pool. Keeping both profiles cost a two-way TLB beside the direct-mapped one, a second profile in every unit, integration, corpus and runner matrix, and a second runtime framework in the app. The 4 KiB profile's larger page map costs about 100 MiB per 8 GiB guest at boot, and its peak footprint under Geekbench was 7.5% higher on the 8 GiB M1 guest and level on the iPad's 2 GiB guest. The host page stays 16 KiB, so the shadow slots, kernel slots, device aperture and ELF alignment did not change. Lesson: G37's gap closed once the 4 KiB path was optimized, so a default chosen to avoid a gap should be re-measured after the gap's causes are worked on. |

| Attempt | Disposition and lesson |

| Measure host instructions per guest instruction | Measured with per-carrier hardware counters and an exact execution histogram. Geekbench single-core costs about sixteen host instructions per guest instruction, almost all in gadget entries; memory transfers are a quarter of guest instructions but the largest share of fast-path cost, and a few hundred hot entries cover nearly every execution. | Count why linked chains return to the executor | Measured. Most executor round trips are indirect-site misses, most of them returns, and most at sites with more targets than any small cell array can hold; each round trip costs a few hundred host instructions. This motivated the return-record ring and G220's target table. |
| Return-record ring for cached returns | Accepted, and unconditional. Linked calls push {return PC, return BlockLink} on a private sixteen-record ring, and a cached return whose cells miss pops a matching record and trains the cell, removing most return misses for a few percent on Geekbench and Aquarium; a deeper ring changed nothing. A first form that skipped cell training lost on monomorphic returns, so the ring must train the cell as the cold helper would. |
| Tagged TLB keys | Rejected, and its switch removed. Keying the software TLB by the address as presented removes the two-instruction top-byte check from every scalar transfer, but Geekbench single-core measured −1.2 percent score and +1.3 percent guest CPU time, and Aquarium −2.2 percent, none positive. As in G186 and G187, the check overlaps the TLB dependency chain rather than extending it; MTE guests would also key several tags of one page into the same set. |
| Fused transfer translation | Accepted, and unconditional. Keying same-page transfers by the page of the final byte while indexing by the first folds the cross-page check into the tag compare, since 16 KiB neighbours never share a direct-mapped set, and trims a few host instructions from every hit path for small Geekbench gains. The compare's operand form mattered: a shifted-operand `cmp` was slower than a separate shift and plain compare. With the count reduced, the remaining transfer lever is latency, not instruction count. |
| Four indirect cells and NOP elision | Four cells: removed; they cover only the minority of misses at three- and four-target sites. NOP elision: rejected, and its switch removed; leaving BTI and PAC hint entries out of the stream was level on a loader-like corpus and Geekbench, because a dispatched NOP is two predicted instructions that retire in the shadow of the gadgets around it. |

## Android boot and memory investigations

| Attempt | Disposition and lesson |
| --- | --- |
| Matched cold/warm platform timing | Early Android startup was not the bottleneck; first-boot ART and package verification dominated. Exact one-off timing tables were not retained. |
| macOS vCPU sweep at 4 GiB | Measured. Eight uniform vCPUs were the boot-time sweet spot on the M1 Ultra host: near-best typical cold boot with much tighter cold-run spread, the best warm median, and no benefit from increasing to ten. |
| Physical-iPad vCPU sweep at 1792 MiB | Measured. Four uniform vCPUs were the viable boot-time sweet spot on the 4 GiB A15 iPad: cold/warm medians were about 79.9/44.0 seconds versus 87.5/47.9 seconds with two. Six vCPUs was not viable at this RAM allocation; iPadOS terminated all four cold attempts under memory pressure before boot completion. |
| Build-time ART placement | Accepted. Ordinary application artifacts are installed beside their APKs instead of being routed to an unbuilt `system_other` image. APEX-contained apps remain a separate platform boundary. |
| ext4 Android payloads and APEXes | Accepted for the Android release; it removed EROFS-specific execution issues without changing Linux desktop rootfs policy. |
| Anonymous host VM for Android RAM | Accepted. It removed file-pager churn from cold boot while preserving ordinary file-backed disk behavior. |
| Full physical-device RAM wiring | Superseded by on-demand commitment. Wiring all of `mem=` at startup charged the whole ceiling to the app's footprint whether or not the guest used it; physical iOS now wires a guest-RAM page only while the guest holds it. |
| On-demand guest RAM with page-cache trim | Accepted. Committing guest RAM on demand and trimming the inactive page cache cut the iPad's footprint peak, about halving a memory probe's, with Geekbench unchanged; the costs are slower cold guest allocation and a reread of trimmed cache, as intended. On iOS, wiring chunks of never-touched anonymous memory gives each chunk a private VM object that `MADV_FREE_REUSABLE` never takes out of the footprint, so the RAM root must be one object before any chunk is wired. |
| Kernel physical window as the Android RAM root | Accepted. The software MMU and host services borrow the kernel's linear window, while protection-specific native mappings remain shared aliases. VM tracking confirmed that the separate full-size root mapping disappeared without creating another resident guest-RAM range; physical devices still require headroom for non-guest memory. |
| Apple asymmetric CPU topology | Rejected and reverted. Public topology/QoS hints could steer carriers but reduced useful Android capacity on the target device. |
| Physical-device boot profiling | Measured. After RAM changes, the remaining gap is dominated by host execution and scheduling service for the full gadget/MMU working set. |
| Expedited RCU grace periods until boot completes | Accepted. Mainline ends the kernel's boot-time expedite when init starts, where the Android common kernel kept it for 20 s. Expediting through `sys.boot_completed` started zygote 1.7 s and reached boot completion 2.7 s earlier in the median of an ABBA series of twelve cold Mac boots. Normal grace periods return after boot, so an idle guest does not interrupt every vCPU for each one. |
| Batch sparse UBD discard | Accepted in both maintained kernel lines. A 512 MiB raw discard collapsed from 16,384 requests to four; matched controls measured about 19x lower wall time on macOS and 10.6x lower wall time on a physical iPad, while reclaiming the same sparse-file allocation. |

## x86-64 gadget engine campaign

Measured on macOS with matched ABBA runs: focused static x86-64 probes in the
guest for the mechanism, then Geekbench 6 single-core at one iteration, which
reproduces the default run's per-workload rates, for the workload. X1 through
X13 were settled together over the full suite; later entries were each
measured against their parent on the workloads their mechanism reaches.

| ID | Attempt | Disposition and lesson |
| --- | --- | --- |
| X1 | Declare the ARMv8.5-A host baseline | Accepted as an enabler with no performance claim. The engine is macOS-only, so every host implements LSE, FlagM, FlagM2 and FRINTTS; the assembled object is byte-identical. |
| X2 | Drop result normalization the host already performed | Accepted. A W-form host instruction writes the whole 64-bit register, so width normalization was a no-op at 32 and 64 bits. 32-bit register arithmetic improved 12%. |
| X3 | Fold away the page-crossing test for single-byte accesses | Accepted. A one-byte access cannot cross a page. Byte memory operations improved 4%, and forcing the check to emit reproduced the previous engine instruction for instruction. |
| X4 | Write the deferred-flag tail as one constant | Accepted. Operation and width are macro parameters and the rest of the tail is zero, so eight bytes are one link-time constant. Compare, set-condition and locked paths improved 4%. |
| X5 | Interprocedural optimization for the translation path | Accepted on workload evidence against probe evidence. Steady-state execution is 0.5% to 2.3% slower, but four Geekbench workloads gained up to 2.75% with none regressing. Probes cannot see a change that only affects translation. |
| X6 | Resolve effective addresses without branching on the descriptor | Accepted. Every address field is a translation-time constant. An always-zero state slot lets an absent base or index be added unconditionally and lets the segment selector index a table; truncation distributes over addition, so the narrow-address mask applies once to the sum. Eight branches become none, the bank loses 13%, and memory operations improved 2% to 12%. |
| X7 | Extract byte sign masks with NEON | Accepted. Four shift-and-accumulate steps replace a loop that ran once per lane. The instruction is 78% faster; it is the core of glibc's SSE2 string routines, which a static histogram of a dynamically linked benchmark cannot see. |
| X8 | Skip the store-side publication and overlap check | First attempts rejected: hoisting the check before the store, gating it on the write-code flag, and gating it on the executable-page bit all measured no gain, because Linux grants READ_IMPLIES_EXEC to binaries without a non-executable stack marking and every data page then qualifies as executable. |
| X8b | Call code publication only when a store can publish something | Accepted. Publication was over half of every guest store's cost, and its call is a lookup that misses whenever the page holds no cached translation; recording that in the write translation, and retiring the translation when a page gains code, turns it into one bit test and made stores about twice as fast. The expensive part of a sequence can be the call it guards rather than the instructions that are visible. |
| X9 | Evaluate branch conditions from the deferred record | Rejected. The premise was that a conditional branch pays full flag materialization, but that routine exits early and is already cheap; adding a pre-check ahead of it made conditional branches 57% slower in two independent implementations. |
| X10 | Fold the recorded operation and width into flag materialization | Accepted. Both keys are constants at the recording site, but the materializer rediscovered them through two run-time chains that reached the commonest cases last. One bounded table branch now selects a body with both folded in, so the width masks, the sign-bit index and the clear mask are immediates. INC 22% faster, SETcc and ADC 18%, CMP/Jcc 15%; File Compression +3.1%, PDF Renderer +3.1%, Horizon Detection +2.9%. |
| X11 | Discard the deferred record in the bit-count family | Accepted. TZCNT, LZCNT and POPCNT define every arithmetic flag and cleared all six immediately after paying to materialize them, and every override any gadget records lies inside that same clear mask. Two stores replace the call, and a TZCNT after an ADD is 69% faster. |
| X12 | Evaluate carry conditions from the deferred record | Rejected, reproducing X9 from the opposite direction. This version replaced the call instead of adding a check in front of it, and carry-condition branches were still 70% slower. The zero-condition shortcut it imitates works because a recorded result is already width-masked and one compare answers it; carry needs the recorded operation, so the shortcut reintroduces the dispatch it exists to avoid, in a gadget rather than in a shared routine. |
| X13 | Stop advertising ERMS | Accepted at the time, superseded by X15. While REP ran as a per-element loop, withholding ERMS moved glibc's string work onto the vector gadgets and gained several percent on string-heavy workloads. A capability claim is a scheduling hint to the guest's own dispatchers, so it is a performance decision as well as a compatibility one, and withdrawing a false claim is only one of the two ways to settle it. |
| X14 | Move a repeated string operation in page-bounded runs | Accepted. REP MOVS and REP STOS paid address resolution, a TLB probe and publication once per element; a run confined to one source page and one destination page now pays them once and moves 64-byte blocks, making byte-granular REP tens of times faster. The first version fell back to the element loop whenever the destination page could hold code, and gained nothing. |
| X15 | Advertise ERMS again | Accepted, superseding X13. Once X14 made REP a block move, REP MOVSB and STOSB outran glibc's SSE2 and AVX2 routines even at short sizes, and withholding ERMS no longer bought anything measurable, so the bit went back for the guests that assume it. |
| X16 | Keep the native chain alive across a code publication | Accepted, and the largest result of the campaign. A code publication reset every link in the address space, and links came back only on translation or on one revalidating hit path, so the engine left an already resident stream every few guest instructions. Re-arming a record whenever its slot is returned, and settling a publication when a run leaves the stream as well as when it follows a link, raised instructions per stream entry about sixfold and the three lowest-scoring workloads by 77 to 134 percent; counting stream entries against retired instructions is what exposed it. |
| X17 | Materialize only the carry for the operations that need it | Accepted. INC, DEC, ADC and SBB called the full flag materializer although only CF is live; a carry-only entry through the same operation-and-width table serves the register forms, since a memory form can fault while the stale bits are still visible. Probes gained about 1.7x and workloads a few percent; the gap between the two is the operations' share of a real mix, which the probe alone could not settle. |
| X18 | Read a condition from the flags the compare already set | Accepted. After a 32- or 64-bit CMP, SUB, TEST or AND computed with the flag-setting host instruction, NZCV already holds the x86 flags, so the builder pairs such a producer with a condition consumer through one variant per condition; parity, the CF-consuming conditions after a logical operation and the narrow widths keep the deferred path, and the deferred record is still written so PUSHF and ADC stay correct. Workloads gained several percent while a probe of pure block transitions regressed: the probe measured the one thing the change does not address, and the workloads outvoted it. |
| X19 | Advertise fast short REP MOVSB | Accepted. glibc reads FSRM separately from ERMS to send short copies to REP MOVSB, and after X14 that claim holds; the workloads neither gained nor lost. As in X15, the burden falls on withholding: a neutral measurement is no reason to route the guest around a path that is not slow. |
| X20 | Widen the indirect-branch site cache from two targets to four | Rejected. Four ways per indirect site sped a probe with four call sites into one callee but left the call-heavy workloads inside their spread, because sites that miss with two ways and hit with four are rare in real code. The mechanism working is not the same as the change paying, the gap X17 crossed in the other direction. |
| X21 | Keep a call in the stream when it publishes a code write | Accepted, and the largest result since X16. A per-site exit census found one instruction responsible for most of Geekbench's exits: a linked CALL leaving because its own return-address push had published a code write. A call now publishes and continues like any store, leaving only when the write lands inside the running block, which sped call probes up to 2.4x and the call-heavy workloads by nearly a fifth. A per-site exit census finds a cause that a per-category one cannot, because the categories were chosen before the answer was known. |
| X22 | Link the block end the fetch window created | Accepted. A block cut short by the fetch window ended in an unlinked fall-through, the largest remaining exit cause; the next address is as static as a jump target, so a block that actually falls through now asks for a link, removing nearly all such exits. Clang, with much the largest code footprint, lost a little to the extra link record per truncated block and was outvoted by the other workloads. |
| X23 | Keep indirect-site training across a link cooling | Rejected. Retaining indirect-site training across a link cooling is safe, but the retained entry hits and then follows a dead link, so the exit still happens one category over; an exit census showed the total unchanged. Moving an exit between causes is not removing it, and a census of causes settles such a change without needing a quiet machine. |
| X24 | Key the indirect-branch tier on the target rather than on the branch | Accepted. A return site belongs to the call that reached it, so a function called from N places has N return targets competing at one site however many ways it has, which is why X20 failed; one direct-mapped tier keyed on the target and shared across the address space holds them all, each entry only a prediction that the follower checks against the guest RIP. Monomorphic shapes pay a few instructions for the hashed lookup, but real code outvotes the probe because a polymorphic RET was paying an exit. |
| X25 | Retire only the translations a code change covers | Accepted. A code change anywhere cooled every link in the address space; replaying the code-change ranges a reader has not seen through the cache's selective retirement, and retiring everything only when the span cannot be described, cut the link exits of browser workloads. The first attempt put a `std::shared_mutex` on the translated code-write path and cost more than the change saved, because libc++'s notifies on every unlock and ten vCPUs serialize; a sequence-stamped lock-free ring replaced it. |
| X26 | Reopen the instruction window instead of leaving at it | Rejected. Checking for interrupts and code changes in the stream at each window checkpoint, instead of leaving, removed half the remaining exits but was flat on Geekbench and slower on Chromium, and the in-stream code-change test proved load-bearing. Exits are not fungible: the cheap window exits re-enter without revalidation, while the ones that remain miss the cache or cool links. |
| X27 | Publish a code write only where some executor caches code | Accepted. Under READ_IMPLIES_EXEC every readable page may hold code, so ordinary data stores kept forcing every executor to revalidate; a per-generation registry of the pages holding cached translations lets the TLB tag a page as code exactly when a translation of it exists, with bounded waits for registration and a change-counter retry for a racing fill. Geekbench single-core rose about nine percent, matching what the profile attributed to publication. The cost of an imprecise publication is not the publishing store but every executor's reaction to it. |
| X28 | Bake registers into a generated scalar bank and read host flags at every width | Accepted. A Python generator emits a register-baked scalar bank whose register offsets and bases are immediates, which the host can forward through as G196 found, and host NZCV at every width widens the fused producers. Geekbench single-core rose 13 percent against the 35 the ceiling bench projected, and the wider fusion added nothing on top. A probe gain must be scaled by the share of real run time it touches before it is a projection. |
| X29 | Record shifts and overlay multiplies instead of materializing | Accepted. Shifts now record their own deferred operation and multiplies overlay carry and overflow instead of materializing flags eagerly, for a few percent. The campaign then stood at 1.42 times FEX on the same kernel, short of the summed projections because each assumed halving work that turned out to be a fraction of the total; FEX still led where vector and memory-transfer cost, not flags, set the pace. |
| X30 | Carry host flags across a flag-neutral transfer | Accepted. A flag-neutral register MOV, MOVZX, MOVSX or LEA between a compare and its consumer now passes the producer's host flags through instead of sending the consumer down the deferred path, roughly halving such loops. A memory destination still ends the fusion, because a store's epilogue compares addresses. |
| X31 | Retire only the TLB entries a mapping change covers | Accepted. Every engine entry cleared the whole software TLB, so a fault-heavy guest re-walked its page tables after every fault; replaying the mapping ranges a reader has not seen, the service X25 introduced, retires only the covered entries and falls back to a clear for large or undescribable spans, for broad gains. |
| X32 | Take the preserved carry from host flags in INC and DEC | Accepted. INC and DEC called the carry materializer to settle the pending producer; when the builder knows the producer, a variant per carry source reads the carry in one condition, nearly halving an INC/DEC loop. The suite barely moved: the mechanism is a large share of very little. |
| X33 | Translate a conditional branch's fall-through inline | Accepted, and the largest single change of the campaign. Continuing the decode past a conditional branch puts its fall-through in the same stream, the arm64 engine's G162, which also speeds the taken shape because blocks grow, and it raised every Geekbench workload and the single-core score by nearly a fifth. |
| X34 | Bias the retirement counter by the dispatch budget | Accepted. Counting retirement from minus the dispatch budget makes each linked edge's budget test the sign of one addition, the arm64 engine's G180 inverted; a small probe gain with less gadget text and a neutral suite is worth keeping. |
| X35 | Run the SHA-256 message schedules on the host SHA unit | Accepted. SHA256MSG1 and SHA256MSG2 map onto the host's SHA256SU0 and SHA256SU1, the latter with its third addend suppressed, making a message-schedule loop several times faster with bit-identical results; Geekbench has no SHA workload. The rounds stay scalar because x86 performs two where the host pair performs four, and SHA-1 stays scalar because x86 numbers its words from the high lane down. |
| X36 | Publish the guest rounding mode for the whole dispatch | Accepted. The dispatch publishes the guest's MXCSR rounding mode into FPCR at entry and on every MXCSR write, so conversions use the mode-relative `frinti` instead of a branch tree, the arm64 engine's G30. The first attempt restored a saved FPCR at leave, but a gadget that faults out of a stack-using sequence reaches leave with a different stack pointer and restored garbage that enabled host FP traps; deriving the restore from FPCR itself needs no saved copy. |
| X37 | Bake the vector register and base into 128-bit transfers | Rejected. A bank baking the vector register and base into 128-bit transfers sped the [base + displacement] shape but not the indexed shape SIMD loops actually emit, while adding hundreds of KiB of text that every workload pays for. X28's lesson in its other direction: a bank entry is worth its text only if it bakes the shape the workload uses, and for vector memory the index is part of the shape. |
| X38 | Fold the page-crossing test into the TLB tag compare | Rejected on correctness before it was measured. An unsigned range check against the tagged page base cannot replace the tag compare and crossing test, because a reset entry then falsely hits a window of addresses above its tag, the null page for the reset tag; comparing the last byte's page instead lets a crossing access hit when the set hash maps both pages together. A tag compare carries an invariant a range check does not, that this entry describes this page, and the invalid entry is where the difference shows. |
| X39 | Leave a NOP untranslated | Rejected. Eliding NOPs sped padded loops but broke the one-retirement-per-architectural-instruction contract that budgeted replay and the K-trace corpus pin, and restoring the count costs about what the dispatch saved. The retired count is the unit of guest progress, not bookkeeping, and an instruction that does nothing is still one. |
| X40 | Discard the pending record in operations that define every flag | Accepted. Instructions that define every flag they do not leave undefined (COMISS and UCOMISS, PTEST, the packed string compares, bit scans and counts, the BMI flag writers) now clear the pending record with two stores instead of materializing it first, while BT and its siblings write their carry into the override; probes gained up to twofold and the workload mean slightly. |
| X41 | Write scalar vector moves with one full-width store | Rejected. Replacing a scalar vector move's zeroing wide store plus narrow store with one full-width store was inside the noise, and the merging register form was slower. This core does not stall on a wide load after a narrow store into the same slot at this distance; the store-forwarding folklore is from other microarchitectures. |
| X42 | Keep AESKEYGENASSIST in the vector unit | Accepted. AESKEYGENASSIST now stays in the vector unit, with RotWord as a shift and shift-insert, a broadcast round constant and zips, instead of crossing to the general registers; a key-expansion probe gained while Ray Tracer, the only workload expanding keys, stayed flat. Kept for the probe and the shorter, transfer-free body; the instruction's profiled share did not survive the suite. |
| X43 | Round PMULHRSW with SQRDMULH | Accepted. PMULHRSW now uses SQRDMULH, with the one lane where both inputs are 0x8000 (x86 wraps, the host saturates) flipped back by a mask built before the multiply so an aliasing output is compared first; probes gained but the suite was flat. With X42 this is the second profiled share that did not survive the suite, so per-gadget attribution is suspect for memory forms and their shared tails. |
| X44 | Give the software TLB four times the reach | Accepted. The direct-mapped software TLB grew fourfold, to 32768 entries in 1 MiB, because image workloads walk more pages than the old table reached; small footprints drift slightly on host TLB reach and the suite gained modestly. The probes understated the suite because a benchmark loop touches its pages in order while a real workload interleaves them with other data. |
| X45 | Park the carry INC and DEC preserve instead of settling it | Accepted. The opening profile ranked candidates by what executes rather than by a static
instruction histogram, and so retired several planned items that barely ran:
truncating FP conversion and scalar FP compare, locked read-modify-write,
gadget-TLB associativity, and specialized shifts and stack fusion. Its largest
item, leaving and re-entering the stream, was not answered by any of the
structures the plan proposed; counting exits by cause found the existing chain
machinery switched off wholesale (X16), and later exit censuses drove X21 to
X26. What remains of stream re-entry is exits that miss the program cache or
re-arm a cooled link, so further work belongs in translation and validation
rather than in the count of returns.

Five lessons generalize. Whether a page might hold code is not a condition this
engine can branch on to save work. An executable-stack declaration has Linux
mark every data page executable, and a probe whose data segment is plain
read-write with a non-executable stack still reported code-capable on every run,
while across the workloads publication actually fires on well under one percent
of self time. The predicate is true far more often than publication is needed
and cannot be predicted from the guest's own markings, so X8's first attempts
and X14's first version both measured nothing by skipping work when it was set.
The work has to become cheaper rather than conditional. Instruction count
inside a gadget is a weak predictor: the changes that paid removed a loop, a
call, or a branch, and those that only shortened a straight-line body did not.
A probe measures the mechanism it names and nothing else, so a candidate that
only affects translation, or that trades throughput for latency, needs a
workload to judge it. And laziness is worth paying for only where retrieving
the deferred value is cheaper than computing it: X9 and X12 failed at the same
boundary from opposite directions, while X10 succeeded by making the deferred
path itself cheaper rather than trying to avoid it. Finally, a profile ranks
what runs but not how often a structure is bypassed; where a fast path exists,
count how often it is taken before assuming its cost is the thing to reduce.

## x86 equality conditions in Wine

| Attempt | Disposition and lesson |
| --- | --- |
| Avoid full flag evaluation for equality-only consumers | Accepted. Register CMOVE and CMOVNE read the deferred zero result while keeping the other pending flags, and adjacent 8- and 16-bit CMP, SUB, TEST and AND consumers reuse the host zero flag, with differential tests preserving the full later flag result. It removed startup work from a timed game launch, which stayed close to its deadline, so it is not a reliability or general speedup claim. |
| Reuse narrow subtraction carry conditions | Accepted. Zero-extended byte and word CMP/SUB operands preserve host unsigned ordering as well as equality. Adjacent carry-dependent branches and SETcc can use NZCV without materializing deferred flags; signed and parity conditions retain the deferred path. Differential checks cover all operand widths and conditions, including narrow sign boundaries. This removes work from startup hash loops without changing guest timers. |

## Layout-sensitive cleanups

Cleanups that move data layout on the dispatch path were held back until
measured against a same-day control, gadget changes on the iPad and JIT changes
on the Mac, and landed only when every probe and workload was neutral or faster.

| Attempt | Disposition and lesson |
| --- | --- |
| Remove the gadget body arena's counting resource | Rejected. The wrapper's only reader had moved to the arena's own count, but it is `ProgramCache`'s first member, and removing it shifted every member's cache-line phase and slowed the bimodal indirect-link probe. The member stays; a dead member at the head of a page-aligned hot structure is layout, not dead code. |
| Drop `ProgramWords`' zero-length inline buffer | Rejected. It removed a word from every program and moved displacements through `ProgramCache::find`, `store` and `run_block` with the bank unchanged; the probes were level but Navigation lost in the Geekbench subset and was level run alone, so the evidence is mixed. The loss appears to follow the translation cache's state after the preceding workload, which steady-state probe loops do not exercise. |
| Pin the gadget region at the head of each image's text and pack the bank | Accepted, with a bank phase of 48. On the iPad a probe's fast or slow mode is chosen per launch, partly by the address dyld loads the runtime at, and where the region sat in the image moved with unrelated size changes and with the automation build's extra code, so a probe's odds changed and the automation build did not stand for the shipped one. Matching a previous layout's 64-byte phases, or even its page offsets, did not reproduce its odds. With the region first in `__text`, helper calls through a veneer island, and section names apart from the x86-64 engine's, every product carries the same region bytes at the same image offset, and the 141 KB of holes kept for removed entries could go. Against 65b22934, gcc, llc, shell and the totals were level. Against current main they stayed within 0.6 percent, with the real-work total 0.37 percent behind, but main's automation build puts its bank at a different 64-byte phase from its shipped build, so that figure partly measures the automation build. The Mac JIT was level. At the inherited bank phase of 16, scalar_mix fell to its slow mode in most launches, so the bank takes 48. After the compile workloads the link probe ran in its slow mode in all but one pinned launch at either phase, about 4 percent behind both references. No window of bits of the region's, the runtime's or the other images' load addresses predicted link's mode better than which build ran, so a lead pad ahead of the region was not tried. Link's mode is set outside the region; pinning controls the gadget region, and probes still swing with layout changes elsewhere in the runtime. |
| Pin the x86-64 gadget region and give each engine group a fixed slot | Accepted. One x86 gadget change moved 38,381 of 41,513 gadgets by 2,480 bytes and cost Geekbench's File Compression and Clang 2 to 4 percent with no change in code, until a pad put every gadget back at its offset modulo 128. The region now starts on a 16 KiB page after the arm64 region, helper calls go through a veneer island, and each of the 60 groups has a slot of whole 128-byte lines at the phase its gadgets had on main, so a group that changes size moves no other group's bytes and one that outgrows its slot moves the later groups by whole lines. Growing one group by 232 bytes left the other 104 slots byte-identical at the same offsets. In three counterbalanced same-day rounds on earlier tips of the pin, the probes' medians stayed within 5 percent of main's either way, apart from a memory probe that followed the host's load, and Geekbench's subset was level where the host was quiet (145.7 for the pin against main's 143.3 at a load below 5); host load of 8 to 60 moved single runs by up to 40 percent, so the figures bound a loss and do not show a gain. Placing the generated baked families last, where main puts them, was a third arm and was level with the island-first order wherever the host was quiet, so the island-first order stays. Unreachable padding is 1.7 percent of the region. A slot's 1 KiB of room did not outlast one shared-macro change: a longer cold path in `effective_address` outgrew 19 cold slots at once, and `fit_slots.py` now lists every slot that no longer fits. A pin that fixes only the region's start leaves a size change moving everything after it; the offset that matters is each group's, and a slot per group fixes it. |
| Read host capability flags from the feature set | Rejected. Replacing twelve duplicated booleans with accessors sped the JIT probes but cost Geekbench's Navigation in every gadget launch, and the change is one commit across both engines. It also moved the gadget bank in the linked framework: a byte-identical bank object does not pin the bank's address, so a cleanup elsewhere in the runtime can still change its cache-line phase. |
| Remove the JIT's unused GPR residency planner | Accepted. Production never planned, but the planner's members made `JitCompiler` large enough that `run_block`, which holds one for a miss, called the stack probe on every entry. Without them the polymorphic-indirect probe ran 15 percent faster, link 7 and syscall 5, with generated code unchanged. A large object on the dispatcher's stack costs every block entry, not just the miss that uses it. |
| Count JIT block links once | Accepted as level. Dropping a counter that always equalled the size left every probe within about 2 percent, with no consistent sign across wall and host CPU time. |
| Recycle the JIT cache when a link table fills | Accepted. Link records keyed by address space were reclaimed only with a full arena, so short-lived processes filled the tables first and later blocks published unlinked exits, a tenfold slowdown a few hundred processes into a launch; recycling the cache on a full table holds steady speed. A bounded table keyed by a transient identity needs a reclamation path of its own, because exhaustion that degrades quietly shows up only as a performance cliff. |
| Fold the arm64 gadget cache's constant epoch into slot state | Accepted as level. ## x86-64 JIT memory order

| Attempt | Disposition and lesson |
| --- | --- |
| Run x86-64 JIT code without x86's store order until the guest kernel marks the mm shared | Accepted. FEXCore's TSO emulation, acquire loads and release stores with barriers around vector accesses, had been on for every address space. Keyed on the kernel's sticky mark, a single-threaded process runs plain loads and stores. On the M1 a memory-bound probe ran 19 to 37 percent faster, a single-threaded `xz -3` 15 percent faster and `gcc -O2` 1 percent faster, with integer, SIMD and x87 probes level. The fences an ordered space puts around REP MOVS and STOS cost nothing measurable. Geekbench 6's worker stays single-threaded but maps `/dev/mem` read-only and shared for a moment to read firmware tables, and the sticky mark then stayed, so its single-core subset ran ordered and measured the ordered path; a thread count alone does not tell which path a process runs. |
| Clear the mm-shared mark when its last sharer goes | Accepted. The mark now follows the mm's live state, so a process that was shared for a moment returns to plain loads and stores, and both orderings of an address space stay cached. Geekbench 6's worker runs ordered only between its `/dev/mem` mmap and munmap; on the M1 its single-core File Compression ran 1.9 times faster, Horizon Detection 21 percent and Navigation and PDF Renderer 10 percent faster, with Clang level. A process that shares and unshares repeatedly pays about 0.1 microseconds per change instead of running ordered from its first share. Removing a process's last shared mapping walks its mappings, about 7 nanoseconds each, so 10,000 of them add 68 microseconds to that munmap; private unmaps and an exec loop are level. The first version worked the mark out in every kernel, and on the iPad's gadget engine the arm64 kernels, whose host never reads it, paid about 4 nanoseconds per guest entry: a getpid loop ran 1.2 to 1.4 percent slower and a process-switch loop 0.6 percent slower, in every ABBA quad. Built only into the x86_64 slice, both loops are level with the old kernel, or faster, since that one still read its sticky mark at every entry. A sticky correctness mark charges its cost to every process that was ever briefly shared, and a per-entry test belongs only in the kernels whose host reads it. |
| Leave accesses through RSP plain in ordered code, as FEXCore does by default | Rejected as unsound; ordered code orders them. Message passing through a variable in a thread's own frame showed the forbidden outcome 1 to 96 times in 200,000 rounds with the exemption and never without it. On the M1 the order costs an ordered process 5 percent on Geekbench 6 PDF Renderer and 3 percent on a four-thread `xz` and on a stack-bound kernel, with the other single-core subset workloads level within their spread. With vector order off the cost was the same, so it is the general-register accesses; vector stack accesses add nothing measurable. |

## Non-streaming SVE in the JIT

The JIT lowered non-streaming SVE at a 128-bit vector length to AdvSIMD, until
the profile was removed (2026-10-11): the SVE-specific entries below describe
code that is gone, and linking the exit of a block cut short by the fetch
window stays. The first checkpoint compared the SVE and NEON guests of
Background Blur, HPL, ChaCha20 and libyuv on an M1 and an M4.

| Attempt | Disposition and lesson |
| --- | --- |
| Check top-byte ignore once per block in SVE memory words | Accepted. The state is the emulator's setting, loaded with the vCPU state, so the block's first check covers its later words. It took the SVE guest of Background Blur from 8.2 to 3.2 percent more cycles than the NEON one. Every JIT memory word carries the check, about 5 percent of Blur's cycles under both guests, so moving it into the translation key would serve all of them. |
| Lower SVE words with a partial governing predicate inline | Rejected before building. An AND with the predicate costs three host instructions on every full-predicate access, and a dual-mode block needs a dispatcher key bit, for at most 1.2 percent of Background Blur, whose library exits cost about 130 ns each. |
| Share one translation among SVE loads of a base | Accepted. An SVE kernel interleaves its broadcast loads with arithmetic, which the NEON same-base runs cannot fuse, and each load paid a full 16-instruction translation. A window proves one page for up to 17 loads within 256 bytes and keeps the host address in the chain state. HPL's SVE guest went from 0.76 to 0.82 of the NEON guest's GFLOPS on an M4. Giving B-element broadcast registers no host binding changed the dgemm kernel by nothing, so register residency is not what limits it. |
| Link the exit of a block cut short by the fetch window | Accepted, and the largest change of the checkpoint. A straight run of more than 64 words is several blocks and each exit returned to the executor, so an unrolled dgemm loop spent half its host instructions there and its chain ended every 85 guest instructions. Linking the exit as a branch does made a 2000x128x128 dgemm 1.75 to 1.9 times faster and HPL 38 percent (NEON guest) and 85 percent (SVE guest) faster on an M4, where the SVE guest now matches the NEON one. ## AArch32 gadget engine

Measured on the iPad's A15 (2 vCPUs, 4 KiB kernel, gadget engine) with
Geekbench 5 armv7 workloads, three launches per arm in ABBA order with a
reinstall between arms, against the arm named in each row (the first row's

workload not named was level. The Mac showed the same orders of magnitude on
the FP rows, which is how the counters below were collected.

| Attempt | Disposition and lesson |
| --- | --- |

## JIT semantic-helper exits

A JIT memory word that falls back to its semantic helper switches the FP
environment to the host's and back, about 14 ns on the M1, and ends its block;
the whole exit cost about 30 ns. GB5 Ray Tracing spent 37 percent of its vCPU
samples in those switches at 4 KiB. Exit counts are per run of a counting
build on the M1.

| Attempt | Disposition and lesson |
| --- | --- |
| Prove a memory run's page from the bytes it accesses | Accepted. A run of unsigned-offset accesses through one base proved and translated the base itself, so a base near a page end whose fields all lay further on failed on every execution and ran its first access in the helper. The run now translates its first accessed byte. Ray Tracing's exits fell from 59 to 3 million at 4 KiB and from 15 to 0.8 million at 16 KiB, and its M4 score at 4 vCPUs rose from 129 to 522 at 4 KiB and from 309 to 642 at 16 KiB. A proof keyed on a stable address fails every time, not occasionally: prove the bytes accessed. |
| Run a memory run that straddles two pages natively | Accepted. A structure across a page boundary failed the run's proof on every execution. A cold path translates both pages and replays the run's accesses, each on its page's host base, and only an access that itself crosses takes the helper. Asset Compression's exits fell from 46 million to the 12 thousand real crossings; Ray Tracing on the M4 rose from 522 to 613 at 4 KiB and from 642 to 669 at 16 KiB. The cold copies were 1.6 percent of Clang's generated words. |
| Run LDAR and LDAPR as host load-acquires | Accepted. Every LDAR ended its block in the ordered helper, and C++ reaches LDARB at every function-local static guard. After the three changes the remaining switches are real page crossings, at most 14 million a run in Object Remover and well under a million elsewhere: under 1 percent of any workload, against a change to the environment code the gadget engine shares. |

The JIT lowers AArch32 in `cpu/arm64/jit/a32`. Geekbench 5 armv7 on it scored
0.466 (single-core) and 0.471 (multi-core) of the aarch64 build, on an M4
MacBook Air with 4 vCPUs. The work was ordered by the dynamic host
instructions per guest instruction of each workload, by the share of
executor returns and by the busy samples in the host FPCR writes. Every row is
a paired comparison of the single-core workloads (two 1-vCPU guests at once on
an M1 Ultra, the order swapped each repetition, rounds above host load 40
dropped), the score of the change over the score before it, unless it says
otherwise. All of them ran Geekbench 5 at its default iteration count; the
repository now runs one iteration, and scores of the two settings do not
compare.

The result, on the M1 Ultra at 4 vCPUs, was 2.0x the armv7 single-core and
multi-core scores of the start, with the aarch64 score unchanged. Measured
against the aarch64 build of that base, the armv7 ratio went from 0.53 and
0.46 to 1.06 and 0.96 (single-core and multi-core), but that build predates

to 0.7x of the M4 on every workload at 4 KiB, at 1 and at 4 vCPUs. Against

to 0.57).

| Attempt | Disposition and lesson |
| --- | --- |
| Keep the host FPCR mode across exits and links | Accepted. A Neon instruction runs under the Standard FPSCR value and a VFP one under the guest's. decode, an execute, a return to the executor and a re-entry.

| Attempt | Disposition and lesson |

| Inline loads with register-offset writeback whose destination is the offset | Accepted. The form was 99% of Horizon Detection's library exits and most of those of the integer workloads. ## AArch32 JIT: memory access

Inline TLB probes are 20% to 40% of the host instructions executed in the
memory-heavy single-core workloads, so a shorter probe is a general saving.

| Attempt | Disposition and lesson |
| --- | --- |
| Probe the 4 KiB TLB in two fewer instructions, skip the crossing test of an access aligned to its size, and fold the crossing test into the tag compare | Accepted as three changes with one measurement: single-core +5.6% over the block before them, 11 of the 12 workloads level or faster (Gaussian Blur 1.11, Machine Learning 1.14, SQLite 1.09, Rigid Body 1.07). The A32 probe is its own copy of the shared sequence: bit i of the index is bit 12+i of the address xor bit 25+i, which is one xor with the address shifted by 13 and one extract, and the tag compare shifts the address itself. Comparing the tag with the page of the last byte makes the crossing test free, because the entry belongs to another page when the access crosses. The tests compare the emitted words, because a probe with a wrong index passes every behavioral test by missing. |
| Add the TLB delta in the access, as `[x9, w8, uxtw]`, instead of before it | Accepted. The add was 2.8% to 5.1% of the executed host instructions and three quarters of the accesses after it have the register-offset form. Single-core +4.8% over the step before it together with the revert of the skip region below (Speech Recognition 1.16, Camera 1.12, Text Compression 1.05). A pair, a multiple transfer, an acquire or release, a lane transfer and a store that records its host range keep the add. |

Camera and Structure from Motion were 2 percent lower in three of four pairs. A branch on guest data costs more than the work it skips. |
| G245 | Run the ADD or SUB that computes a VLD1 or VST1 copy's base inside the copy | Accepted. ## AArch32 gadget stream size

Each row shrinks what the AArch32 gadget builder stores per instruction.
Stream words come from a static sweep that translates Geekbench 5 armv7, the
Debian armhf release's /usr and eleven F-Droid armeabi-v7a apps without
running them; cache memory from a census of Geekbench 5 armv7 single-core at
131,072 slots with eight ways. Timing is Geekbench 5 armv7 with one iteration
against the commit before each row: single-core in concurrent 1-vCPU guests on
the Mac's benchmark pool, three repetitions with the order swapped, and
single- and multi-core at 4 vCPUs on the M4 MacBook Air, two mirrored
repetitions (G259 to G261 have the Mac pool only). Together G251 to G254
took 16.6 percent of the A32 stream words and 16.2 percent of the T32 ones,
and 8.2 percent of the single-core cache (15.0 percent of its streams) at an
equal hit rate. Merged onto main 5acb130c, which carries G243 to G250, they
ran Geekbench 5 single-core 0.8 percent faster than main on the Mac pool over
three repetitions (0.2 and 0.4 in the two that no host burst touched) and
AES-XTS 2.1 percent faster in an AES-only series. G259 to G261 took a further
3.0 percent of the A32 stream words and 2.4 percent of the T32 ones; merged
onto main 2c5a854f, they shrink the single-core cache 1.2 percent (2.6
percent of its streams) at an equal hit rate and run Geekbench 5 single-core
0.5 percent faster than main, faster in each of three repetitions. Both sets
leave the AArch64 gadget parts byte-identical.

| G251 | Fold a PC register operand into the immediate form | Accepted. PC as an unshifted register operand is a constant: the second operand of any data-processing instruction, the first of one that commutes, SUB and RSB swapping, and the base of [PC, Rm]. The builder emits the immediate or offset form with the PC value instead of a three-word scratch write. A fold whose other operand is its destination, as in position-independent code's `add rX, pc, rX`, takes the indexed immediate entry rather than G196's in-place one, because an indexed load writes the literal it adds to and indexed loads consume the sum: through the in-place entry AES-XTS ran 4 percent slower than before the fold, its samples there half as many again as in the two gadgets the fold replaced, and through the indexed entry 2.1 percent faster. Stream words -2.5 percent in A32 and -3.4 in T32 (a static sweep of GB5, Debian armhf and F-Droid armeabi-v7a code); GB5 single-core cache streams -1.5 percent at an equal hit rate (Mac, 131,072 slots, eight ways). GB5 level: M4 MacBook Air 4 vCPUs single-core +0.9 and multi-core +0.9 percent over two mirrored reps, the loaded Mac pool 1 vCPU -0.8 (three ABBA reps, within its noise). A fold that reaches a specialised entry inherits that entry's assumptions about its neighbours. |
| G252 | Take a linked call's LR from its return link | Accepted. A BL or BLX whose return address is linked follows that link, and the link's key is the return address with the instruction set in bit 62, so the linked and cached call gadgets derive LR from it and the builder drops the LR word from those forms; the unlinked forms keep it. Stream words -2.3 percent in A32 and T32 (the same static sweep); GB5 single-core cache total -1.9 percent and streams -1.7 at an equal hit rate. GB5 level: M4 MacBook Air 4 vCPUs single-core -0.1 and multi-core +0.5 percent over two mirrored reps, the loaded Mac pool 1 vCPU -0.7 (three ABBA reps, -0.4 without one leg that a host burst halved on one workload). |
| G253 | Let the memory transfers carry a position instead of a key | Accepted. The single and doubleword transfers, VLDR, VSTR, TBB and TBH pushed their instruction's key only so that a fault could name the instruction; like the exclusive and ordered gadgets, they now carry its position in the block in their operand word, and a fault reports the position, which the executor resolves to the key. Stream words -12.3 percent in A32 and -11.0 in T32 (the same static sweep); GB5 single-core cache total -5.5 percent, bodies -9.4 and streams -12.1, at an equal hit rate. GB5 level: M4 MacBook Air 4 vCPUs single-core -0.5 and multi-core -1.0 percent over two mirrored reps (a rep spread of 2.7 points in multi-core), the loaded Mac pool 1 vCPU +1.2 (three ABBA reps). A word kept only for a fault costs every translation that holds it; the position a fault needs fits in spare operand bits. |
| G254 | Fold a MOVT into the MOVW before it | Accepted, small. A MOVT right after an unconditional MOVW of the same register outside an IT block replaces the top half of the MOVW's constant, so the pair is one move of the whole constant and the MOVT's gadget and operand leave the stream. Stream words -0.12 percent in A32 and -0.18 in T32 (the same static sweep) and GB5 single-core cache streams -0.1 percent at an equal hit rate: the pair is rare in this code, which loads most constants from literal pools. GB5 level: the loaded Mac pool 1 vCPU 0.0 percent (three ABBA reps), M4 MacBook Air 4 vCPUs single-core -0.4 and multi-core -1.4 over two mirrored reps whose multi-core spread was 2.4 points, for a change confined to the builder. |
| G255 | Recompute a block transfer's library operand instead of storing it | Not built. The semantic-library operand that LDM and STM carry for the page-crossing path is about 0.7 percent of A32 stream words and 0.8 of T32, and that of VLDM, VSTM and the Advanced SIMD structure transfers 0.3 and 0.45 more. The library could rebuild it from the instruction word, but reaching that word at run time means finding the block's body from the stream position or fetching the guest word by a key the gadget no longer carries, which couples the library to the program cache. Under one percent of the stream does not pay for that coupling. |
| G259 | Let the block transfers carry a position instead of a key | Accepted. LDM, STM, PUSH and POP pushed their instruction's key for a fault and for the semantic-library call of a transfer that crosses a page. | G260 | Pack a block transfer's registers five bits each | Accepted. The registers were CpuState byte offsets, eight to a stream word after the first operand word.
